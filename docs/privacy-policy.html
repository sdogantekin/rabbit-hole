RabbitHole Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 19, 2026

RabbitHole ("we", "us") is a mobile app for discovering Wikipedia articles through a swipeable card feed, with quiz and gamification features. This policy explains what data we collect, why, and what choices you have.

Data we collect

When you create an account, you can either sign in with an email (via a one-time code) or continue as an anonymous guest, in which case we don't collect an email address at all.

  • Account data: email address (email sign-in only), a display name, an optional profile photo, and your app language setting.
  • Usage data: which articles you swipe on and in which direction, your liked/saved articles, your interest categories and how they're weighted based on your activity, quiz sessions and scores, and streak/XP/level stats.
  • Sharing data: if you choose to share a quiz, we store a record of who has played it and their score, so a leaderboard can be shown.
  • Consent preferences: your choices for product analytics and whether your name appears on leaderboards (both described below).

We do not collect your location, contacts, payment information, or any advertising identifiers. We do not show ads.

How we use it

  • To personalize your feed based on what you've liked and skipped.
  • To generate quiz questions from articles you've liked.
  • To track streaks, XP, levels, and badges.
  • To show a leaderboard for quizzes you or others have shared.
  • To improve the app, if you've opted in to product analytics (off by default beyond basic crash reporting).

Who we share data with

  • Supabase — our database, authentication, and file storage provider, hosted in the EU (Frankfurt, Germany).
  • Anthropic — when generating quiz questions, we send the text of articles you've liked (not your account details or identity) to Anthropic's Claude API.
  • Wikimedia/Wikipedia — we fetch article content from Wikipedia's own APIs. No data about you is sent to Wikimedia.

We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with advertisers or data brokers.

Leaderboards and visibility

Your name is only shown to other people on a quiz leaderboard if you've turned on the "Public leaderboard" setting in the app (off by default). If you haven't, you still appear on a leaderboard for a quiz you've played, but anonymized.

Data retention and deletion

We keep your account data for as long as your account exists. Cached Wikipedia article content (not your personal data) is automatically refreshed and expires after a few weeks.

You can permanently delete your account and all data tied to it — liked articles, swipe history, quiz results, streaks and XP — at any time from Profile → Privacy & consent → Delete account in the app. This is immediate and cannot be undone.

You can also request deletion, or ask what data we hold about you, by emailing us directly (see Contact below).

Security

Data is encrypted in transit (HTTPS/TLS). Our database uses row-level security so that, by default, only you can read or write your own data.

Children

RabbitHole is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect data from children under that age.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy from time to time. We'll update the date at the top of this page when we do.
